## Who this guide is for

This guide is for parents and guardians who are deciding whether to step into travel or club sports, or who are already in it and wondering if they are doing it well. Maybe your child just got an invitation to a tryout. Maybe the schedule has taken over your weekends. Maybe you are on your third kid and still have questions.

It does not matter which sport your child plays. Most of what families run into is the same everywhere: the schedule, the costs, the tryouts, the pressure, and the question of whether a program is any good.

## Why it exists

Much of what parents hear about travel and club sports comes from people who have something to sell, or from other parents who are just as unsure as you are. The terms are confusing, the costs are rarely spelled out, and the stakes can feel high for a child who just wants to play.

This guide exists to slow that down. It tries to give you the questions to ask, the things worth noticing, and a calmer way to make decisions for your child and your family.

## What this guide is, and what it isn't

**What it is.** A practical, parent-level guide. It explains the words, describes what is usually involved, and points out what to ask and what to watch for. It is written to help you think, not to tell you what to do.

**What it isn't.**

- **Not affiliated with anyone.** First Team Parents is not connected to any league, club, team, tournament, or governing body, and it does not speak for any of them.
- **Not legal advice.** If you are dealing with a contract, a dispute, or a question about your rights, talk to a lawyer.
- **Not medical advice.** If your child is hurt or something seems wrong, see a doctor or other health care provider and follow their guidance. The [Safety](/safety) chapter is about what parents can watch for and ask about, not about diagnosing or treating anything.
- **Not financial advice.** The [Costs](/costs) chapter helps you see what a season can really involve. It cannot tell you what is right for your budget. Only you and your family can decide that.

Every program, league, and family is different. Treat what you read here as a starting point, then check the details with the people actually involved.

## How to use the chapters

You do not have to read the guide front to back. Come in where your question is.

- **Just getting started?** Begin with [Start here](/start-here) for what rec, travel, club, and showcase mean, then [Costs](/costs).
- **Choosing between programs?** Go to [Choosing a program](/choosing-a-program) for questions to ask and red flags to look for.
- **A tryout is coming?** Read [Tryouts](/tryouts).
- **Wondering what this is doing to your family?** Read [Family life & burnout](/family-life-and-burnout).
- **Want to know what to ask about your child's safety?** Read [Safety](/safety).
- **Heard a word you don't know?** Check the [Glossary](/glossary).

Several chapters have checklists. They are meant to be used. Print one, take it to a parent meeting, or read it in the car before a tryout.

## Written by a parent

First Team Parents is written by Jason Anderson, a parent of three kids, from elementary school through high school, near Birmingham, Alabama.

What you will find here is written from a parent's point of view, with the aim of being honest about both the good parts of travel and club sports and the hard ones.

## Questions, corrections, and topic ideas

This guide gets better when parents say what it's missing. If something is unclear, out of date, or just wrong, or if there is a topic you wish someone would explain, please reach out: [suggestions@firstteamparents.com](mailto:suggestions@firstteamparents.com).

When you write, keep your child's details and other families' details out of it. A general description of the situation is plenty.
